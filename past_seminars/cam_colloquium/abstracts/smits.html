Turbulent Flows on Large, Fast Vehicles

Alexander Smits
Department of Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering
Princeton University

Abstract: The performance of vehicles such as submarines, ships, airplanes and missiles depends strongly on the effects of Reynolds and Mach number on the fluid mechanics of the external and internal flows. Large vehicles always means large Reynolds number, but they are typically designed at Reynolds numbers much lower than the full-scale. Extrapolation to full-scale conditions is not wholly reliable, and this sometimes leads to unwelcome surprises, which may necessitate an expensive re-design. Fast vehicles also means large Reynolds number, but Mach number effects can also become important. For supersonic and hypersonic vehicles, they become critical. We have very little information on the behavior of turbulent flows at high Mach number, especially when Reynolds number is an additional variable. In this seminar, the effects of Mach and Reynolds number on turbulent flows is illustrated using recent research results obtained at Princeton, and the implications for design procedures is discussed.
