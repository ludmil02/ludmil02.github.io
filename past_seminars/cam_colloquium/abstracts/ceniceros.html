Effective and Computational Chaos

Hector D. Ceniceros
Department of Applied Mathematics
California Institute of Technology

Abstract: It is well known that surface tension plays an important role in the pattern formation and finger selection of dendritic solidification of a multi-phase flow. Most of the work to date has been on steady states and their linear stability. However, in actual experiments the interface does not evolve into a steady state when surface tension effects are very small. Instead, noise perturbations excite instabilities which lead to the formation of convoluted interfacial patterns. The ill-posedness of the zero-surface tension problem and the singular nature of surface tension give rise to challenging new difficulties for numerical simulations. Even noise at the round-off level can quickly grow and affect the solution. This extreme noise sensitivity makes it very difficult to identify the asymptotic behavior of the interface in the limit as surface tension tends to zero. Here, we design a novel numerical method to study the zero-surface-tension limit of a Hele-Shaw expanding bubble. Using a compact parametrization and rescaling of the governing equations, we derive an effective method that allows us to separate the effect of noise from that of surface tension, and to capture the singular contributions of extremely small surface tensions. Our numerical results demonstrate convincingly that the zero-surface-tension limit is indeed singular. Moreover, the onset of the singular effects of surface tension is well predicted by the linear theory. However, nonlinear interactions become very strong near the time when the linear theory breaks down. The curvature becomes very large at a later stage, suggesting that the complex singularity gets very close to impinging on the physical domain. Our computations also indicate that surface tension defines a length scale in the finger width.
